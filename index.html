How the simulator works

Move the electorate

Economic and cultural shifts range from −1 to +1. Positive economic shifts move voters toward market liberalism; positive cultural shifts move them toward traditionalism. Variance scales both axes around the national baseline median by its square root.

How seats are decided

Each of 400 single-member districts elects the party closest to its median on the compass. As in the original model, this is a Condorcet/Ranked Pairs interpretation under Euclidean preferences and centrally symmetric district electorates. The app does not synthesize individual ranked ballots.

Small local differences

The 50 district types retain their original centres and counts. Each district receives a fixed, paired offset of at most 0.10 compass units by default. Every type’s average stays at its original centre. Offsets are capped at 80% of the distance to the closest baseline party boundary, preserving the original baseline of 186–170. Offsets do not change when you run another election. Set variation to zero to reproduce the shared-centre model.

National votes

First-preference shares integrate the existing three-Gaussian national electorate over nearest-party regions across the full plane. The baseline economic marginal median remains 2.0. The national mixture and geographic district model are calibrated separately.

Random elections

Each draw starts from the same baseline. Economic shocks are normal with SD 0.35; cultural shocks have SD 0.25. Out-of-range draws are rejected and shifts rounded to two decimals. Log variance is normal with mean 0.04 and SD 0.20, truncated to [0.50, 2.00]. This retains the requested density equality f(V) = f(1/V). This condition is distinct from equal probability in reciprocal intervals.

Run saves the current preview. Random election draws one seeded shock. Draw N elections restarts the chosen seed, generates N fresh shocks, and adds them to history. Editing party positions is an exploratory scenario; the original calibration applies to the default positions.
